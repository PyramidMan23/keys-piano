Three ways to make the keys feel alive

Every direction below is running live in the panel, play it, don't read about it. Same engine, same 4 ms effects budget, same colour-blind rule: shape and position carry the meaning, colour is garnish. The difference is what happens in the 300 ms after your finger lands.

What each one actually draws

Written as build specs, not adjectives, so the cost is arguable before a line ships.

Recommended

Liquid glass

Notes fall onto black water stretched across the keyboard.

Per strike
Two or three expanding elliptical strokes at the contact point, one compressed white glow beneath, and 6–10 droplets thrown upward. A chord shares one wider ripple rather than stacking three.
How the hit reads
A razor-white bead lands first, then the ripples spread for 220–300 ms. Perfect gets a filled bead; anything else gets a hollow one, readable without colour.
Faked depth
Ellipses flatten and widen as they grow; the far arc draws dimmer and thinner than the near arc, so the surface reads as a plane you are looking across.
Cost
Low, a handful of stroked ellipses on top of what already renders.
Risk
If ripple lifetime creeps past ~300 ms it becomes the trails effect an earlier review already rejected.

Aperture light

Each correct key briefly opens a slit into something very bright.

Per strike
Three nested rounded rectangles in perspective, one white core, one horizontal flare, and 4–8 straight rays clipped to the width of that key.
How the hit reads
Snaps open in 40 ms, holds 60 ms, collapses over 160 ms. Perfect opens three frames; early or late opens one, displaced to that side.
Faked depth
Frames shrink toward a vanishing point above the key, with line weight and alpha falling off as they recede.
Cost
Very low, plain paths and clipping, no gradients, no blur.
Risk
Rays taller than ~80 px turn into the rejected full columns, and it is the coldest of the three.

Fog apparition

Contact condenses a brief luminous form that carries the verdict.

Per strike
Three to five pre-rendered irregular alpha clouds drifting up 20–45 px at diverging scales, plus one sharp bead. No ambient fog between hits.
How the hit reads
The bead flashes instantly, then the fog resolves into a word-shaped plaque and evaporates inside 500 ms.
Faked depth
Layered sprites at different scales, speeds and overlaps, genuine parallax, the most physically convincing of the three.
Cost
Medium, additive overdraw during dense chords; reduced mode would drop to 1–2 sprites.
Risk
Softness is the direct opposite of the razor-bright contact rule, and the effect outlives the moment that caused it.

Cinema ✦

Why Rousseau and Patrik Pietschmann look the way they do, the closable half, built.

What the videos have
A glossy black deck that reflects the notes, bloom around every bar, embers drifting off the strikes, a vignette pulling your eye to the middle, and offline rendering: their frames take minutes each in After Effects, ours get 4 milliseconds, live, while judging your timing.
What this mode adds
True black ground, zero grid, no lane lines, no beat lines, a whisper of a hit line: every photon in the frame belongs to a note. The deck goes dark glass and mirrors each arriving note, bars get hot cores and wide bloom, strikes throw slow embers, a vignette frames it. Locked flat 2D.
What stays impossible in a browser
True gaussian bloom over the whole frame, motion blur, film grading, and their content: a virtuoso playing dense four-octave runs. Half of "their look" is simply that the music is spectacular.
Cost
Measured live in the readout below the stage, watch it while you play.
Risk
This deliberately breaks the earlier restraint ruling (ambient embers, persistent glow) so you can judge with your own eyes whether restraint was right.

Early · Perfect · Late

The Guitar Hero moment, built so it still works when the colours don't.

◀◀ Early > 120 ms ahead
◀ Early 45–120 ms ahead
◎ Perfect within 45 ms
▶ Late 45–120 ms behind
▶▶ Late > 120 ms behind

Three signals, none of them colour: the word, the direction the chevron points, and where it appears, left of the key for early, right for late, dead centre for perfect. Doubling the chevron past 120 ms means the size of the miss reads at a glance too.

How it would ship

Four gated waves, each one provable on its own.

  1. Perspective lanesConvert the flat note lanes to the converging projection you're looking at above. Pure geometry, no new effects, no budget change. This alone is most of the "3D" feeling.
  2. Liquid-glass contactReplace the current bloom with the ripple-and-bead strike. Measure the frame cost against the existing 2.31 ms worst case before anything else lands.
  3. Early / Perfect / LatePromote the existing signed timing data into the chevron plaque at the key, keeping the tick strip as the running history.
  4. Menu and shelfCarry the same depth and glass into the library so the app stops changing personality between screens.
  • ✱Nothing here breaks the colour-blind rule. Every verdict is a word plus a direction plus a position.
  • ✱The 4 ms effects budget still governs. Any direction that blows it eight frames in twelve latches to reduced mode, exactly as it does today.
  • ✱Spectacle stays at the contact moment. No ambient haze between notes, that was rejected once already and it is still the right call.
