## Context

"Keys" is Mark's personal piano-learning app at C:/Users/markh/keys-piano. Songs
live in js/songs.mjs as note arrays: { b: beat, d: duration, m: midi, h: 'L'|'R' }.
The `h` field decides which hand the app teaches him to use: it colours the
falling notes, drives hands-separate practice, and picks the staff in score
view. If `h` is wrong, the app teaches him the wrong fingering.

I audited all 128 songs (tools/hand-audit.mjs). Findings:
- 3 songs where one hand never plays at all
- 7 songs with CROSSED HANDS (a right-hand note sounding below a simultaneous
  left-hand note)
- 24 songs where one hand leaps more than 2 octaves inside a single beat
- 20 songs where one hand is asked to span more than 17 semitones AT ONCE
  (worst: 29 semitones simultaneously)

The cause is visible in the per-hand pitch ranges: e.g. `in-the-end` has left
C2-E3 and right D3-B5; `interstellar` has left F1-B2 and right C3-E7. That is a
PITCH THRESHOLD split, not an arrangement: everything above a fixed line was
called "right hand", so the right hand inherited both the melody and the inner
voices and became unplayable. Correctly-arranged songs in the same file (Fur
Elise) do not have this.

## What I propose to do

NOT rewrite the 24 song definitions by hand (I would be inventing arrangements).
Instead: one normalisation pass applied to SONGS at module load, which re-derives
`h` ONLY for songs that fail the playability audit, and leaves correct songs
untouched. The audit then runs as a gate over the exported SONGS.

The algorithm, per song:
  - group notes by onset beat
  - keep a running "hand position" for each hand (the pitch centre of what that
    hand last played, so a hand stays where it was)
  - for each onset group, sort the simultaneous pitches ascending and consider
    every split point k (lowest k notes to the left hand, the rest to the right)
  - score each candidate split and take the cheapest:
      * hard penalty if either hand's simultaneous span exceeds ~12 semitones
        (an octave; 14 with a small penalty for a stretch)
      * penalty proportional to how far each hand must MOVE from its running
        position, so lines stay in the hand that was already playing them
      * very large penalty for a move greater than about 2 octaves inside one
        beat, which is what makes the current data unplayable
      * a small penalty for leaving a hand idle when it could take notes
  - update each hand's running position from what it just played
